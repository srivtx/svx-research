## 1. Headline findings

1. **The vacuum is real, and it is bottom-heavy.** A would-be builder on Reddit r/InventoryManagement (q01, undated) specifies the gap verbatim: something for "small CNC shops and machinists — more capable than a spreadsheet, but without the complexity of a large ERP." Mid-market ERP pricing locks out exactly that buyer: Epicor Kinetic runs "a platform fee, commonly cited around $1,500 a month, plus $100 to $200 per user per month" (octurasolutions.com, undated 2026 page), with "typical contract values … Epicor's is $100K–$500K against Odoo's $10K–$80K, and Epicor charges $100/user/month" (erpresearch.com, Jan 15, 2026); Infor CloudSuite Industrial sits "around $150–$300 per named user per month" (rfp.wiki, q12, 2026). The accessible legacy end (E2/JobBOSS) is *hated* — see §2.
2. **But the middle is already served — "build a better job-shop ERP" is not an open gap.** ProShop ("consolidated ERP/MES/QMS scope for machine shops … Paperless shop-floor model supports steady …" — rfp.wiki, q19, 2026), Fulcrum (real-time production tracking, quality, inventory — softwarefinder.com; compared against Genius ERP and Odoo on sourceforge.net, q10), Odoo (cheap, but job-shop job costing "is finding a partner with experience to bring all of the various components into your job cost calculations" — ssibtr.com, q21b), Katana (ecommerce-platforms.com, Apr 13, 2023 — positive; "best pick for small distributors" — gaugius.com, Aug 30, 2026), Fishbowl ("strongest overall pick for Spare Parts Stock Management … with Odoo and NetSuite rounding out" — gitnux.org, Jul 21, 2026). Shop owners are actively comparison-shopping (Practical Machinist thread title: "ERP - JobBoss² vs. ProShop vs. Something Actually Good?" — Mar 30, 2023).
3. **Excel is the real MES.** A build-vs-buy consultancy post (blog.digitalheroesco.com, Milwaukee, undated) describes the dominant deployment pattern: "the install sits there as a system of record while the actual scheduling happens in a shared Excel file and a whiteboard near the press." For estimating, "Excel or Open Office Calc are what most people use" (practicalmachinist.com, Jul 25, 2024). Older but consistent: an Intuit survey found "84% used a combination of pen and paper, and spreadsheets to manage inventory" (via cpapracticeadvisor.com, Dec 8, 2022 — pre-2024 stat). What fills the space between shelfware ERP and the Excel/whiteboard truth today is *custom services*, not products: "Wollongong fabricators and manufacturers build custom BI dashboards for quote-win rate, job margin, machine use and material variance, past a Power BI" (digitalheroesco.com, q04).
4. **Shop-floor data capture is a graveyard of funded vendors — killed as a category.** MachineMetrics ("from $149/mo. $99 each. Pricing follows a contact-sales model … No free plan" — itechguides.com, Sep 22, 2026; "volume-based per connected machine" — softwr.com), Guidewheel (a $59/month price with free version/trial listed on a SourceForge comparison page, q09 — attribution between the two products ambiguous), Datanomix, SensrTrx, Mingo, Scytec DataXchange ("MachineMetrics (81/100) vs Scytec DataXchange (62/100)" — mfgtechreview.com), L2L and Epsilon3 ("The best MachineMetrics alternatives are Kinetic, Epsilon3, and L2L" — g2.com), plus a second wave of digital-traveler vendors (ifactoryapp.com, May 26, 2026: "Operators clock onto jobs, log scrap with reason codes, and declare downtime in 5 seconds"; paperwise.com, Sep 14, 2026; workerbase.com, May 23, 2025; advantive.com, Oct 26, 2020; proshoperp.com, Mar 22, 2021). Nothing in the evidence targets sub-10-machine shops' price points, but nothing shows that gap either — that residual is UNVERIFIED, not open.
5. **Compliance paperwork is the strongest surviving wedge.** Aerospace/defense buyers demand "material certificates, lot traceability, inspection records, first article inspection, revision control" (uneedpm.com, q13); "Documentation requirements for aerospace components create administrative burdens" (shamrockprecision.com, q13); AS9102 first-article inspection is a formal buyer requirement at supplier after supplier (precisionam.com, Sep 24, 2026; mfgbase.com; wsmtechnology.com; goscpm.com). Layered on top: CMMC rulemaking commenters argued costs "were underestimated, particularly for small businesses" (regulations.gov, Dec 26, 2023), and a 20-employee machine shop typically has "no dedicated IT person" (localitdept.com, q14). What exists is enterprise QMS (ETQ Reliance — rfp.wiki, q21) or FAI-as-a-feature inside shop software ("generate the report from the same source data as the inspection" — industrialmonitordirect.com, Sep 17, 2026). No SMB-priced compliance-packet generator surfaced in 29 searches.

## 2. What small manufacturers run, cost, and hate (legacy incumbents)

**Evidence.** JobBOSS² "starts at $3,000/year, built specifically for small job shops" (easyloanguide.online, Jun 14, 2025 — *weak source, a loan-guide content site; treat as unverified*). Sentiment: "Can't DISLIKE job boss and e2 enough. Do yourself a favor and run away. So buggy and inefficient in my experience. My shop left them." (Reddit user reposted on instagram.com, q01b, undated); "JobBoss customer support is below average at best … They're all grossly overpriced and …" (practicalmachinist.com, Mar 30, 2023); "It has been the worst implementation in my 45 years of implementing manufacturing software systems. Our customer service rep is late or non responsive" (softwareadvice.com JobBOSS² reviews, q02); "E2 has major scheduling issues with releases that we can't overcome … lack of quality customer support, job costing" (community.spiceworks.com, Nov 1, 2012 — **older**, but echoed by the 2023/2025 complaints). Installed base is real: job listings ask for "ERP/MRP systems (E2/JobBOSS, Epicor, etc.)" skills (jobtoday.com, q01b). Counter-evidence for honesty: one Jan 2025 review is glowing — "JobBoss does an excellent job of tracking job time and costs. It is seamless from the Quote to final job cost" (softwareconnect.com, Jan 7, 2025) — so the hatred is not universal; support/implementation is the recurring failure mode, not core job-costing.

**Why the field is open at the edges.** Pricing ladders force rationing at the small end: an SMB running Epicor Kinetic reports "We don't like the cost of Epicor Kinetic Mobile Warehouse. For our 12 barcode scanners, we are going to purchase 4 licenses" (epiusers.help, Dec 4, 2022) — per-device licensing literally removes scanners from the floor. G2 reviewers add "licensing for add on programs locks you in" and MES "licensing can cause errors" (g2.com Kinetic reviews, q11). Support is the churn driver: "Support responsiveness is a recurring pain point in public review commentary" (rfp.wiki, Kinetic vs Fishbowl, q11), and an SAP partner runs a three-part series on "why some small and midsize manufacturers running Epicor begin evaluating alternatives" (clientsfirst-us.com, Mar 17, 2026 — vendor-flavored but confirms the exodus dynamic).

**Tried & failed.** "Rip out the legacy ERP" is the incumbent playbook (ProShop, Fulcrum, Odoo partners, SAP B1 pitches). Rip-and-replace loses to shelfware: the digitalheroesco post (q04) documents the outcome — the new system becomes a passive system of record while Excel keeps running the shop.

**AI economics.** Weak for a replacement-ERP play; the hate is real but served by an active middle market. Skip.

## 3. Where Excel sits between the systems (quoting → job costing → scheduling → shipping)

**Evidence.** Scheduling: "the actual scheduling happens in a shared Excel file and a whiteboard near the press" while the ERP "sits there as a system of record" (blog.digitalheroesco.com, q04). Quoting: "Excel or Open Office Calc are what most people use" for estimating (practicalmachinist.com, Jul 25, 2024). Inventory: 84% of product-based businesses on pen/paper + spreadsheets (Intuit survey via cpapracticeadvisor.com, Dec 8, 2022 — **older**). Job costing: even Odoo — the cheapest credible option — needs "a partner with experience to bring all of the various components into your job cost calculations" (ssibtr.com, q21b); partner content flags "configuring Odoo modules, mapping resources, setting up work centers, and ensuring real-time …" as the hard part (bistasolutions.com, Mar 10, 2026). Reporting: shops buy custom BI for "quote-win rate, job margin, machine use and material variance" (digitalheroesco.com, q04). Adjacent corroboration of spreadsheet stickiness: 74% still run IFRS 16 compliance in Excel "despite the standard being in effect since 2019" (ifrstech.com, Apr 7, 2026 — finance domain, not manufacturing).

**Why the field is open.** Nobody productizes the layer *between* the ERP and the Excel/whiteboard truth. The current fix is a consultant building a one-off dashboard. Field-service shows the gating pattern to avoid: "Job costing plus inventory … gated above Starter" (rfp.wiki on ServiceTitan vs Service Fusion, Aug 8, 2026 — different vertical, same SMB pricing wound).

**Genuine fix shape.** An overlay, not a system: read-only ingestion of the shop's actual sources (ERP exports, the live scheduling workbook, quote PDFs, invoices) → computed per-job quote-vs-actual margin, quote-win rate, machine utilization. Never ask the shop to rip anything out; absorb the workbook rather than replace it (this is the manufacturing instantiation of registry gap #12, Excel absorption layer — cross-reference, do not duplicate).

**AI economics.** Good: quote/PO/invoice PDFs and messy workbooks are exactly the unstructured-to-structured translation LLMs newly made cheap (SVX translation thesis).

**Caveat.** Evidence density here is 2–3 primary sources (one consultancy anecdote + forum threads + adjacent stats). This candidate survives but is the *weakest-evidenced* survivor; it needs a verification pass before building.

## 4. Shop-floor data capture: paper travelers vs cheap digital (KILLED as a category)

**Evidence.** The paper-traveler pain is documented across a decade: "massive costs from outdated paper job travelers" (proshoperp.com, Mar 22, 2021); "Using paper for SFDC is not only less efficient … generates a lot of …" (advantive.com, Oct 26, 2020); "faster time to get the job info to the floor … maintaining accurate, up to date info is easier" (practicalmachinist.com, Nov 14, 2016 — **older**); "Operators clock onto jobs, log scrap with reason codes, and declare downtime in 5 seconds. Paper travelers go away" (ifactoryapp.com, May 26, 2026); "A digital traveler or electronic job packet replaces the physical document" (paperwise.com, Sep 14, 2026); "Reduced Downtime Higher Productivity" (machinemetrics.com, Oct 20, 2022).

**Verdict: KILLED.** The vendor layer is dense and funded: MachineMetrics, Guidewheel, Datanomix (slashdot.org, q09), Scytec, SensrTrx (g2.com, Sep 12, 2026; upkeep.com, Oct 6, 2020), Mingo (sourceforge DELMIAworks-vs-Mingo, q16), L2L/Epsilon3 (g2.com, q09), Augury (softwaresuggest.com, q16), MaintainX (fabrico.io, Feb 24, 2026), plus the entire ERP-adjacent wave (ProShop's "paperless shop-floor model," rfp.wiki q19). Pricing has come down toward SMB (Guidewheel at $59/mo on SourceForge — attribution ambiguous; MachineMetrics "from $149/mo, $99 each" — itechguides.com, Sep 22, 2026; older G2 review via aws.amazon.com, Oct 29, 2019: "learning curve, the cost can be a little high"). A machine-monitoring "one-liner": vendor hype-cycle marketing already exists (mingosmartfactory.com, May 30, 2024). Residual (sub-10-machine shops) is UNVERIFIED — no evidence either way.

## 6. Job-shop estimating and quoting (Paperless Parts et al.)

**Evidence.** Paperless Parts is "the leading estimating and quoting software for job shops and contract manufacturers" (todaysmachiningworld.com, Nov 8, 2022; mmsonline.com, Mar 22, 2023), "Boston-based … founded in 2017 … secure, ITAR-compliant cloud quoting and sales platform" (apis.io, undated). Competitors/adjacents: OX Quote CNC Machining (sourceforge.net, q12b), digifabster ("automated quoting software, CAD file analysis, and 3, 4, 5-axis machine support. **Verdict: split.** The professional-estimator segment is KILLED — Paperless Parts owns the wedge and is ITAR-aware. But the floor is untouched: "Excel or Open Office Calc are what most people use" (practicalmachinist.com, Jul 25, 2024 — *after* Paperless Parts was "leading"), and a shop owner asks "Website for my shop with instant quoting?" while crowdsourcing metal-removal rules of thumb ("raw material weighs 8 lbs, at $3/lb for metal removal, quote of 4 3/4 lbs = $14.25 is pretty close" — practicalmachinist.com, Jan 31, 2024). Bottom-of-market instant/assisted quoting is UNVERIFIED: Paperless Parts' actual pricing never appeared in the evidence, so "who's still priced out" is unknown.

## 7. Quality/compliance documentation: the surviving wedge

**Evidence (demand side).** Buyer requirements are non-negotiable: "material certificates, lot traceability, inspection records, first article inspection, revision control" (uneedpm.com); AS9102 FAI is "the formal proof that a supplier's manufacturing process produces a part that conforms to every requirement on the drawing" (mfgbase.com); "AS9102 — First Article Inspection requirements that apply to new or changed parts, demanding documented evidence" (wsmtechnology.com); suppliers advertise AS9102-format FAI reports as a differentiator (precisionam.com, Sep 24, 2026; industrialmonitordirect.com, Sep 17, 2026). The cost side: "Documentation requirements for aerospace components create administrative burdens" (shamrockprecision.com). Compliance stacks on compliance: CMMC commenters flagged underestimated costs "particularly for small businesses" (regulations.gov, Dec 26, 2023); "extensive documentation, reporting, and government audits … can be daunting" (technologyblog.rsmus.com, Oct 9, 2024); a defense-capable CISO "commands $175,000 to $250,000" (carlbjohnson.com — vCISO pitch, vendor-flavored); compliance "can represent 10 to 20 percent of program budgets" (blackwingspace.com — **vendor claim, satellites context, unverified**); the archetypal buyer is "a small machine shop … with 20 employees and no dedicated IT person" (localitdept.com).

**Why the field is open (provisionally).** What surfaced on the supply side: enterprise QMS (ETQ Reliance vs Odoo — rfp.wiki, q21) and FAI-as-a-feature inside a shop-software suite (industrialmonitordirect.com: "generate the report from the same source data as the inspection"). Paperless Parts covers the *quoting* side of ITAR, not the quality-packet side. No SMB-priced, standalone "produce the buyer's compliance packet" product appeared in 29 searches. **But** R6 never ran the dedicated kill-search (existing FAI/ballooning tools of the DISCUS/InspectionXpert class did not appear anywhere in the evidence — absence of evidence is not evidence of absence).

**Genuine fix shape.** Overlay + document assembly: ingest the inspection data (CMM results, caliper measurements), the drawing (auto-balloon), the PO/material certs (OCR), and the traveler; emit the AS9102/PPAP forms, the cert package, and the revision-controlled audit trail. Per-job or per-packet pricing, not per-seat. Distribution must be bottom-up — and note the category-identity trap: "not a lot of job shops … consider themselves job shops. They might consider themselves a mom and pop …" (mfgbroadcast.com, Episode 68) — sell "paperwork," never "QMS/MES/ERP."

**AI economics.** Strong: drawing ballooning, cert OCR, form population, and revision reconciliation are document-translation problems — precisely the SVX translation thesis. Compliance is also the rare case where the buyer (the shop's owner, defending a revenue contract with an aerospace/defense customer) *is* the sufferer — no buyer≠user mismatch at 20 employees, which is why bottom-up forum distribution can work here.
